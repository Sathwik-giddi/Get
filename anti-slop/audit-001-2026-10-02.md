## Findings

### 1. Em dashes in shipped text — R-02 — HIGH

`README.md:3`, `README.md:4`, `README.md:16`, `README.md:167`, `README.md:170`, `ui/app.py:63`, `ui/app.py:183`, `ui/app.py:199`.

Eight occurrences. R-02 bans the character outright, and here it appears in a cluster with the other AI tells: forced triads and a generic sign-off. Replace with commas, colons, or a full stop.

### 2. No design direction, and the result is the sterile default — R-37 — HIGH

There is no `DESIGN.md`. `ui/app.py:30-32` is untouched Streamlit: default blue-grey surfaces, default sans, default radii, three unrelated status colors picked from a familiar set. Swap the product name and it could be any dashboard. This is the Part 1 "Sterile Default" pattern, which the core names as a direction failure, not a filter failure.

A `DESIGN.md` draft accompanies this audit for approval.

### 3. Mode colors fail or barely pass contrast — R-25 — HIGH

Measured with `antislop-human/contrast-check.py` against Streamlit's `#ffffff`:

| Color | Role | On `#ffffff` | On `#f0f2f6` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `#d97706` mock | `ui/app.py:32` | 3.19:1 (large-text pass only) | 2.84:1 **FAIL both** |
| `#16a34a` gemini | `ui/app.py:32` | 3.30:1 (large-text pass only) | 2.85:1 **FAIL both** |
| `#2563eb` vertex | `ui/app.py:32` | 5.17:1 pass | 4.31:1 large-text only |

`st.metric` renders its value at roughly 2rem, so the 3:1 large-text bar is the correct one on the main surface, and both amber and green clear it by a thin margin. On any secondary surface they fail outright. This is the "Grey-on-Grey Hallucination" failure mode in a different costume: the pairing was never measured. The proposed palette in `DESIGN.md` passes 4.5:1 on both surfaces.

### 4. Empty and error states do not tell you what to do — R-27 — HIGH

`ui/app.py:105` renders the audit table's empty state as "No runs yet." That says the table is empty, not why it is empty or what fills it. `ui/app.py:39` and `ui/app.py:43` surface a raw status code and a raw `requests` exception string. Neither names the next action. This is the Part 1 "Placeholder Empty and Loading States" pattern.

The loading state at `ui/app.py:129` is the one that passes: it names what is happening.

### 5. Decorative emoji carry no information — R-04 — MEDIUM

`ui/app.py:30` `page_icon="🐇"`, `ui/app.py:65` warning icon, `ui/app.py:165` uses 🎯 for `make_decision` and 🔧 for every other skill, `ui/app.py:182` uses ✅ / ⚠️ / • for the three evidence stances.

🎯 and 🔧 are the generic AI glyph vocabulary: they mark "important" and "a tool", not "the only skill allowed to decide" or "perceptual extraction". ✅ and ⚠️ also signal status by hue alone, which the human checklist rejects on its own. Replace with typographic marks that carry the same distinctions, so the distinction survives for a color-blind reader and in forced-colors mode.

### 6. Status colors are an unmotivated palette — R-01, R-29 — MEDIUM

`ui/app.py:32` mixes amber, green and blue with no system behind them, and the page has no accent at all. Three colors doing three unrelated jobs is not a palette. The accent should mark the one moment that matters on the screen, which here is the decision.

### 7. Header copy is a forced triad and a signpost — R-16, R-36 — MEDIUM

`ui/app.py:51`: "It plans, selects skills, executes them, and shows its work." Three verbs listed to sound complete, then a fourth item that restates the product name's promise. `README.md:3-5` does the same thing. Replace with a sentence that tells the operator what the screen is for.

### 8. Python comments — antislop-code — PASS

Scanned `agent/`, `api/`, `demo/`, `tests/`, `ui/`: zero comments. No decorative separators, no workflow narration, no empty labels, no vague TODOs, no end markers. The only comment-shaped text left is tool directives (`# type: ignore[return-value]` in `agent/gemini.py:107-113` and `agent/core.py:130`, `# noqa: E402` in `api/main.py:13-15`), which are instructions to the type checker and linter, not prose. No action.
